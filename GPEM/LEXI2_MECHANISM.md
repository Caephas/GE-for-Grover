## What it shows (plain statement)

The size tiebreak engages almost immediately: the fire rate jumps from ~1% at generation 0 to ~43% at generation 1 and passes 50% by generation 2 (overall 0.545), reaching ~0.66-0.79 across generations 4-10 before plateauing near 0.85-0.93 in mid-to-late evolution. So size pressure does act early, before fidelity is secured in most runs, consistent with the 'premature parsimony' concern. However, the rate is not elevated in the runs that failed to converge: it is no higher than the converged rate and lower at essentially every generation (e.g. 0.694 vs 0.817 at gen 8; 0.817 vs 0.929 at gen 20); the association, if any, runs opposite to the hypothesis. The data therefore support the claim that size-aware selection does real work from the first generations, but do not support the claim that extra early size pressure is associated with non-convergence; if anything, the association points the other way.
